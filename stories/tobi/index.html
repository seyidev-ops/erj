From an automated “no” to Selected at micro1.

In August an automated screen turned Tobi down for a sound-engineering role. Eight weeks later, micro1 selected him for its Voice Actor/Coach role. Here is what happened in between.

Eight weeks, step by step.

  1. 31 Aug
    He sent us AUDIT.

    Asked what usually happens after he clicks Apply, he answered: “I apply and mostly hear nothing” and “I keep getting rejected.” He ran the free diagnosis and sent his CV.

  2. 31 Aug
    The rejection that explained everything.

    Deepdub had turned down his Sound Engineer application after an automated questionnaire. The skill was there. What the screen read was not: his CV was not carrying the words and details it was checking for.

  3. 7 Sep
    The part to fix.

    Not his talent, his representation: the CV and portfolio had to show plainly that he could do this work for a remote team.

  4. Sep – Oct
    He reworked the CV.

    Using our suggestions on his earlier CV, he rebuilt how his audio, sound and voice work read to a screen and to a person.

  5. 16 Oct
    He applied to micro1.

    The Voice Actor/Coach role lists sound engineering, audio engineering and audio-quality evaluation among its skills: work he could now show.

  6. Oct
    Selected.

    micro1 marked his application Selected. His words: “Thank you for your suggestions on my earlier CV, again.”

What the role pays

micro1 lists this role at $30 to $70 an hour. Work on micro1 is project-based and hours depend on the projects available, so we do not turn the rate into a weekly or monthly figure. What we can say: the rate is in dollars, and the work is done from where he lives.
