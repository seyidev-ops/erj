What to Write in Your LinkedIn About Section When You Have No Remote Experience Yet

The blank box that stops everyone. A four-paragraph structure you can fill in tonight, plus a full example for someone changing field entirely.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 10 July 2026 5 min read

The About section is the box under your headline. Most people leave it empty, and empty reads as either lazy or invisible. It is also the easiest part of the profile to fix, because you are the only person who has the information.

Two things to get out of the way

Write in the first person. "I help teams..." not "Chidi is a professional who...". Third person reads like a press release about a stranger.

Do not apologise. No "although I have no remote experience", no "seeking an opportunity to prove myself". Nobody hires an apology. State what you can do.

The four-paragraph structure

Paragraph one — what you do, plainly. One or two sentences. The same information as your headline but in a full sentence, so a human reads it comfortably.

Paragraph two — the proof. Two or three concrete things you have done, with numbers where you have them. This is where the credibility lives.

Paragraph three — the remote part. Name the tools you use and how you work with people you cannot see. Even if this came from a hybrid job, a university project or volunteering, it counts.

Paragraph four — what you are open to. One line. What kind of role, and how to reach you.

A full example

I am a customer support specialist. I spend my days making sure that when someone has a problem, they get a clear answer quickly and do not have to ask twice.

For the last three years I handled roughly 60 tickets a day for a Nigerian fintech, across email, live chat and phone. I brought our average first-response time down from about eight hours to under two by rewriting our reply templates and reorganising the queue by issue type rather than arrival time. Customer satisfaction stayed above 90 percent through that change.

Most of my team worked from different cities, so almost everything ran through Slack, Zoom and a shared Notion knowledge base. I am comfortable writing things down clearly enough that nobody needs to schedule a meeting to understand them, and I am used to being trusted to manage my own day.

I am looking for a remote customer support role with an international team. Best way to reach me is a message here or chidi.okafor@gmail.com.

Notice what that did

It never claimed international experience. It described real work honestly, then translated it into the language a remote employer cares about: written clarity, tools, and working without supervision. That translation is the whole job.

Your work already contains most of what an international employer wants. It has just never been described in their vocabulary.
