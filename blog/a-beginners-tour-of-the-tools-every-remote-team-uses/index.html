A Beginner's Tour of the Tools Every Remote Team Uses

Slack, Zoom, Notion, Trello, Loom, Google Workspace. What each one is for, the etiquette nobody explains, and how to practise before you are hired.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 20 July 2026 6 min read

You will be expected to know these on day one. Nobody will train you, because in a distributed team everyone assumes everyone else already knows. Here is the tour, including the unwritten rules.

Slack — the office corridor

Group chat organised into channels by topic. Replaces most of what would be corridor conversation and short emails.

Rules nobody tells you: reply inside a thread rather than the main channel, or you flood everyone. Do not send "hi" and then wait — put the whole question in one message. Use @here sparingly; it notifies everyone online and irritates all of them. Set your status when you step away.

Zoom, Google Meet, Teams — the meeting room

Functionally similar. Learn: joining muted, screen sharing a single window rather than your whole desktop, and recording with the room’s permission.

The etiquette: camera on unless the team says otherwise, mute when not speaking, and if your connection is struggling, say so in the chat rather than freezing silently.

Notion — the filing cabinet that thinks

Documents, wikis, and simple databases in one place. Most remote companies keep their handbook, processes and project notes here.

What matters: being able to create a clean page with headings, link between pages, and use a database with filters. That is the whole beginner requirement.

Trello, Asana, ClickUp, Jira — the work board

Cards representing tasks, moving through columns from to-do to done. Whichever one a company uses, the concept is identical.

What managers actually notice: whether you move your cards without being asked, and whether your card has enough detail that someone else could pick it up. Nothing signals unreliability faster than a board that is out of date.

Loom — the meeting you did not have to schedule

Records your screen and your face and gives you a link. Enormously valued in distributed teams, because a three-minute video at 9pm your time replaces a meeting that would have cost four people an hour.

Use it for: walking through something visual, explaining a decision, giving feedback with tone attached. Keep it under five minutes.

Google Workspace — the paper

Docs, Sheets, Drive, Calendar. The bar is higher than people expect: comments and suggestion mode rather than editing over someone’s work, sensible sharing permissions, and in Sheets, at minimum VLOOKUP or XLOOKUP, pivot tables and conditional formatting.

How to practise before anyone hires you

All of these have free tiers. This weekend:

  1. Create a free Slack workspace, make three channels, practise threading.
  2. Build a Notion page for your job search with a database of applications.
  3. Make a Trello board for the same, moving cards as things progress.
  4. Record a three-minute Loom explaining your portfolio. Watch it back. Record it again.

That last one is uncomfortable and it is the most useful hour in the list.
