AI Wrote Your CV and It Shows: What Screeners Notice

Using AI on your CV is fine. Sending what it gives you unedited is not. The six signs a reader spots, and how to use the tool properly.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 20 November 2026 3 min read

Recruiters now read a great many CVs that were plainly written by a chatbot in one go. They are not rejected for using AI. They are rejected because they all say the same thing, and none of it can be checked.

You can use AI well. First, here is what the unedited version looks like to the person reading it.

Six signs a reader notices

  1. The same verbs everywhere. Spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, fostered. No one talks like this about their own job.
  2. No numbers. "Significantly improved efficiency" with nothing to show how much.
  3. Claims that fit any job. "Results-driven professional with a proven track record." Swap the name at the top and it still reads the same.
  4. Skills you cannot discuss. The tool added "stakeholder management" and "data-driven decision making". In the interview, you are asked for an example of each.
  5. Facts that are slightly wrong. A team size that was never mentioned, or a tool you have not used. AI fills gaps with plausible guesses.
  6. A summary longer than the evidence. Five lines about what kind of person you are, and two thin bullets per job.

Why this costs you more than it used to

When every applicant can produce a polished paragraph in ten seconds, polish stops meaning anything. What still stands out is detail only you could know: the number of branches, the name of the system, the month the backlog was cleared. That is your edge as an experienced person, and a generic rewrite removes it.

How to use AI properly

Give it the facts first. Before you ask for any writing, type out what you actually did in plain words, with rough numbers. The quality of what comes back depends on what you put in.

Ask for questions, not prose. Try this: "Here is my role. Ask me ten questions that would help turn these duties into measurable achievements." Answer them honestly. Your answers are the raw material for the CV.

Ask for options, then choose. Request three versions of one bullet. Pick the plainest one and put it in your own words.

Use it to compare. Paste the advert and your CV, and ask which requirements are not covered. That use is more valuable than any rewriting.

The read-aloud test

Read every line aloud. If you would not say it to a colleague, change it. Then go through each bullet and ask whether you could talk about it for two minutes in an interview. If not, it goes.

Never let it invent

A CV with a made-up certificate or a tool you have not touched will fail at the first practical test, and remote employers test. Delete anything that is not true, however good it sounds.
