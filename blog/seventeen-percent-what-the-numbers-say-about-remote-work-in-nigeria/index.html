Seventeen Percent: What the Numbers Say About Remote Work in Nigeria

About 17% of Nigerian jobs are remote, against roughly 28% worldwide. What that gap means for an experienced professional searching from Lagos, Abuja or anywhere else.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 12 November 2026 3 min read

In July 2026, Vanguard reported on how remote work is reshaping Nigeria's workforce. Three figures from that report are worth sitting with if you are looking for a remote role this quarter.

The three figures

  • About 17% of Nigerian jobs are remote, according to the MyJobMag 2025 Remote Work Statistics Report cited in the piece. The global average it quotes is around 28%.
  • The ICT sector made up 11.31% of Nigeria's real GDP in the first quarter of 2026, up from 10.59% a year earlier, on National Bureau of Statistics data.
  • The World Bank counts about 17.5 million online freelancers across Nigeria, Ghana and South Africa, with Nigerians a significant share.

What the gap tells you

Seventeen against twenty-eight means remote work is still less common here than elsewhere. It also means the remote roles that are open to you are mostly not Nigerian roles. They are posted by companies in other countries who are willing to hire from anywhere, or from Africa specifically.

That changes where you search. A Nigerian job site is built for local hiring. The roles you want sit on company career pages, global job boards and a handful of platforms that say plainly who may apply. We keep a filtered list of them on the Remote Job Board, every one open to someone living in Nigeria.

What employers in the report said

One technology lead quoted in the article put it this way: local employers are now competing in a global talent market. A digital strategist in the same piece said practical skills and portfolios matter more than degrees.

Read those two together. Companies abroad are hiring Nigerians, and what they look at is proof of work. Not the certificate, not the years. Something they can open and judge.

The barriers the report names

The article lists the usual ones: skills gaps, unreliable power and internet, payment problems, and limited awareness of how global recruitment works. Notice which of these you control.

  • Power and internet are costs you plan for. An inverter or a second data line is a work tool, like a laptop.
  • Payments are a question to settle before you sign, not after. We covered it in Getting Paid From Abroad.
  • Awareness of how global hiring works is the one most people miss, and the cheapest to fix.

How global hiring differs

If your last job search was local, three things will be different.

  1. Software reads your CV first. Tables, photos and two-column layouts that look fine to a Nigerian HR officer can come out scrambled.
  2. Eligibility is decided by the location line. "Remote" often means remote within one country. You have to read for it.
  3. They test the work. A short task or a recorded answer is normal. Your examples need to be ready.
