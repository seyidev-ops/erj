Day One: What the First Week of Foundation Training Looks Like

Cohort 13 starts tonight. For anyone deciding about January, here is what the first week of Foundation Training actually asks of you.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 30 November 2026 3 min read

Cohort 13 begins tonight at 8:00 PM WAT. If you are thinking about the January cohort, the most useful thing we can do is show you what the first week is really like. Not the brochure. The work.

Why we start with how you work, not with your CV

Most people come to us wanting to fix their CV. We get there, in Stage 4. We start somewhere else because of something we have seen over many cohorts: people who get a remote job and then lose it within months usually lose it for the same reasons. They could not structure an unsupervised day. They went quiet when they were stuck. Their manager did not know what they had done until someone asked.

Stage 1, the Remote Mindset Blueprint, fixes that first. A CV that gets you hired into a job you cannot hold is not a win.

What you do in the first week

  • Plan your working week in blocks. Not a to-do list. Actual time on a calendar, with the deep work protected from everything else.
  • Write your daily measures. What does a good day look like in your work, in numbers? Most people have never written this down, and remote managers ask for it.
  • Send an end-of-day report. Three short parts: what you finished, what is blocked, what is next. You will send one every class day, and you will get feedback on them.
  • Audit your workspace. Power, internet, the backup when either fails, noise, light for calls. Not a lecture: a checklist you complete and submit.
  • Use AI to plan, honestly. How to use it to organise your week and check your writing without handing over your judgement.

By the end of the stage you submit your Personal Remote Operating System. A person marks it and tells you what is ready and what is not.

How much time it takes

The live class is one hour on weekdays, or ninety minutes on the weekend track. Plan for roughly the same again for the work you submit. Most people do it in the evening after their day job. It is real effort, and it is meant to be. The habits are the point.

The private audit

During the programme, your facilitator goes through your job search with you privately: the role you are aiming for, the applications and interviews you have had, and your current CV. You come out of it knowing which part of your search to work on first, rather than following a generic plan.
