Four Joints, One Leak: Why Your Job Search Is Not Working

Every stalled remote job search is a pipeline leaking at exactly one of four joints. Find yours and you stop wasting months fixing the wrong thing.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 4 August 2026 6 min read

Most people treat a stalled job search as one big undifferentiated failure. It is not. It is a pipeline, and pipelines fail at joints.

There are four, and an offer only comes out when all four hold.

01 · Supply — you can't apply for a job you never saw

Before anything else, real openings have to reach your eyes. If you are refreshing the same three sites, seeing roles that closed weeks ago, or wading through offers that ask you to pay for training first, this is your leak.

Nothing else you do matters while this joint is broken. You can have a perfect CV and aim it at nothing.

What fixing it looks like: a continuous, verified inflow of roles actually open to people applying from Africa. Our free WhatsApp job board exists for exactly this, and it costs nothing. Do not pay anyone to solve your supply problem before you have tried the free version.

02 · Representation — if your CV can't be read, you were never really in the running

You apply and hear nothing back. Not a rejection, not an acknowledgement. Silence.

That silence usually means one of three things: your CV cannot be read by the software that screens it, your CV can be read but does not say what you can actually do, or there is nothing outside the CV — no LinkedIn worth finding, no portfolio, no evidence.

This is the most common leak, and the most misdiagnosed. People respond to silence by applying harder. Applying harder with a broken signal simply produces more silence, faster.

How to check in one minute: run the free 10-point CV self-scan. It scores the same ten things a screening system checks and names the specific failures. It runs on your own device and uploads nothing.

03 · Aim — applying everywhere isn't the same as applying where you'd get hired

You are applying. A lot. Fifty, a hundred, two hundred applications. Still nothing.

Volume is not the problem here — direction is. The roles are wrong for your level, the companies do not hire from your region, or you are firing everything into portals where applications go to die rather than to the humans who make decisions.

Aim is where effort and outcome separate most violently. Twenty well-aimed applications will beat two hundred scattered ones, every single time.

04 · Conversion — interviews don't pay you, a signed offer does

The replies come. Sometimes the interviews come. The offers do not. Or one arrives and the money is wrong because nobody ever taught you what to say when the number is named.

This is the last metre, and it is the one people prepare for least. They spend six weeks perfecting a CV and twenty minutes preparing for the conversation that decides the salary.

The diagnostic

Read these four sentences and find the one that describes your month:

  • "I cannot find real openings." — Supply.
  • "I apply and hear nothing." — Representation.
  • "I apply constantly and nothing lands." — Aim.
  • "Interviews come, offers do not." — Conversion.

One of those will feel more true than the others. That is your leak. Fix that joint and only that joint for the next three weeks.

The reason this matters so much: patching the wrong joint feels like progress. You rewrite your CV for the fourth time while your actual problem is aim, and another month goes. The diagnosis is not an academic exercise — it is the difference between a month spent and a month wasted.
