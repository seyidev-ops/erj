From Intent to Offer: The Whole Method, Written Down

Eighteen chapters, six appendices, one idea: information is not the result. What is in the book, who should not buy it, and why every chapter ends in a task.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 22 September 2026 6 min read

The book is published. From Intent to Offer: How to Get a Remote Job is out as an instant download at ₦15,000.

It is the method we teach in the live cohorts, written to be executed by one person alone. Eighteen chapters across seven parts, six appendices, 171 pages, and every chapter ends with one task to execute rather than a lesson to remember.

The one idea

You probably do not need more information about remote work. You have watched the videos, saved the job posts, joined the groups and asked an assistant the same question fourteen different ways. And the result has not come.

That is not laziness and it is not a closed market. It is that information has not yet become execution, and execution has not yet become a result. Those are three different things, and most remote-work content on the internet stops at the first.

How it is built

Seven parts, following the chain every successful search follows whether the person knows it or not:

Intent — what result you are actually trying to get, named precisely. Evidence — what you have done so far, counted rather than felt. Diagnosis — which of the five parts is breaking. Execution — the repair for that part, and only that part. Result — what exists afterwards. Next result — the loop. Outcome — the signed offer and the first ninety days that keep it.

Chapter 6 is the one people will come back to: five questions, twenty minutes, on paper, alone, and you leave with a sentence — my first leak is ___ because ___ — and the chapter number that repairs it.

The appendices are the part we expect to be used most. The First Fourteen Days is one task a day, an hour or less each, nothing to buy. There are templates for the messages people freeze on, the trackers that turn a feeling into a reply rate, a glossary of the words adverts use without explaining, and a full list of every free and paid ERJ route with prices.

Who should not buy it

Three people.

Someone who will not execute it. This is a book you work, not one you read. If you already know you will not do work nobody is watching, it will join the courses you never finished, and the live cohort exists precisely because some people need a room and a deadline.

Someone whose occupational skill is the gap. No chapter here closes that. Build the skill first; the free Capacity Audit will tell you in ninety seconds whether that is your situation.

Someone who already has the Self-Learn Pack. You have the method. We are not going to sell it to you twice.

What it does not promise

No book can promise you a job, and this one says so on its copyright page. It carries the method more than 446 professionals have been trained on and over 382 have been placed with. Whether it produces a result depends on your skill, your market and the work you actually do.

What it will do is stop you repairing the wrong thing for another six months.
