No Portfolio? Three Things You Can Build This Weekend That Count

"Show us your work" stops people who have only ever worked inside someone else's systems. Here is what to build instead, by function, in two days.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 14 July 2026 6 min read

The problem is real. If you spent four years inside a bank, a hospital or a government office, everything you produced belongs to them, most of it is confidential, and none of it is shareable.

So you build something. Not a fake job — a small, honest demonstration that you can do the work. Employers accept this readily, because what they are actually testing is whether you can produce something finished.

The rule that makes it work

One page of context around every piece. What the problem was, what you did, what changed. A file on its own says nothing. A file with three sentences of reasoning around it says you think.

Customer support

  • Pick a product you actually use. Write five support replies to realistic customer complaints about it — a refund request, an angry user, a confused beginner, a bug report, a feature request you have to decline.
  • Write a one-page help article for something in that product that is genuinely confusing.
  • Build a short macro library: eight reply templates with notes on when to use each.

Virtual assistance and operations

  • Build a full travel itinerary for a fictional three-city business trip, with costs, timings and contingencies.
  • Create an inbox management system in a document: rules, folder structure, response priorities, escalation.
  • Set up a project board in Trello or Notion for a real event you organised, even a family one, and show the structure.

Writing and content

  • Three articles on a topic you know well, published free on Medium or LinkedIn. Published matters more than perfect.
  • A rewrite: take a badly written product page, rewrite it, and show both versions side by side with your reasoning.

Design

  • Redesign one screen of an app you use, and write two paragraphs on what you changed and why.
  • A small brand set for a fictional business: logo, colours, type, one poster.

Data and analysis

  • Take a free public dataset, ask one clear question of it, and answer it in a single chart with a short written conclusion.
  • Build a spreadsheet dashboard that tracks something real — your own expenses, a small business you know.

Software

  • One small working thing, deployed so it can be clicked, with a readme explaining what it does and the one hard decision you made building it.

Where to put it

A single Notion page or Google Drive folder, set to "anyone with the link can view". Test the link in a private browser window before you send it anywhere — a broken permission setting is the most common self-inflicted wound here.

Then put that link in your CV under your name, and in the Featured section of your LinkedIn profile.

Be honest about what it is

Label practice work as practice work. "Self-directed project" is a normal, respected phrase. Presenting a personal project as paid client work is the one way to turn this from an asset into a liability.
