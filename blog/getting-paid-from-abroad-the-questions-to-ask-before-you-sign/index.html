Getting Paid From Abroad: The Questions to Ask Before You Sign

You can do the job well and still lose a slice of every month to fees, delays and exchange rates. Here is what to ask about payment before you accept an offer.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 9 December 2026 3 min read

For many people, the first remote offer brings an unexpected question: how will the money actually reach me? It is worth asking early, because the answer affects how much of your pay you keep and how predictable it is.

A note before we start: payment providers, their fees and the rules that apply to them change often. We are not naming a best option here, and this is not financial advice. Check current terms yourself before you choose.

How remote pay commonly reaches Nigeria

  • Through an employer of record's payroll, often paid into a local account, sometimes in naira, sometimes in another currency.
  • Through a payments platform that companies use to pay contractors in many countries. You get an account on the platform and withdraw from it.
  • By direct international transfer to a domiciliary account in your name.
  • Through a freelancer marketplace, if you found the work there, with its own fees and withdrawal rules.

The questions to ask the employer

  1. Which method do you use to pay people in Nigeria today? Have you paid someone here before?
  2. In which currency is my pay set, and in which currency will it arrive?
  3. On what date each month will I be paid, and how long does it usually take to arrive?
  4. Who pays the transfer or platform fees?
  5. If a payment fails, who do I contact and how quickly is it fixed?

The first question is especially useful. A company that has paid someone in Nigeria before has already solved the problems you are worried about. A company that has not may still be fine, but expect the first payment to take longer.

What to work out for yourself

  • The real amount. Take the offer figure and subtract the fees you will pay to receive and withdraw it. That is your number, not the headline.
  • Tax. If you are a contractor, you are generally responsible for your own tax. Set aside a portion from the first payment, not the fifth. A tax professional can tell you what applies to you.
  • A buffer. First payments are often slow. Have enough to cover your first month without relying on it arriving on time.

Red flags

  • A "company" that wants you to pay a fee to receive your salary.
  • A request for your bank login or card details "to set up payroll".
  • An offer to pay you through a personal account of someone at the company.
  • Any arrangement that asks you to receive money and send part of it on to someone else.

Legitimate employers do not ask for any of these. If you see one, stop.
