Remote Work Tools Ranked by How Much They Spy on You

Your employer may be watching more than you think. Here's exactly which tools do what — and what your legal rights are before you sign anything.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 30 May 2026 6 min read

What Your Employer Can Actually See

Before signing any remote employment contract, you need to know what the tools in your tech stack can actually monitor. This is not paranoia — it's due diligence.

Maximum Surveillance — Know Before You Sign

  • Hubstaff: Screenshots every 10 minutes, keystroke logging, mouse activity tracking, GPS if mobile. Completely visible to management in real time.
  • Teramind: Records full screen video, tracks app usage, flags "anomalous" behaviour patterns. Used in high-security sectors.
  • ActivTrak: Productivity scores, website blocking, detailed activity logs. Often installed without explicit employee disclosure.
  • InterGuard: Email monitoring, social media tracking, file transfer logs. Borderline in some jurisdictions.

Moderate Tracking — Industry Standard

  • Microsoft Teams: Read receipts, presence indicators, meeting attendance auto-logged. Microsoft Purview can allow admins to read DMs.
  • Slack: Message retention, channel activity logs, admin access to private messages in paid plans. More than most people realise.
  • Google Workspace: Document access logs, login times, file sharing activity. All visible to your admin.

Minimal Footprint — Mostly Safe

  • Zoom: Meeting attendance and duration only. No screen recording without your consent in most regions.
  • Notion (company workspace): Page view logs for admins. Content visible to workspace owners.
  • Loom: View analytics only. No background tracking.

Three Things You Should Know

  1. If it's on a company device or company network, assume it can be monitored.
  2. In most countries, employers must disclose monitoring in your contract. Check yours — it's usually in the IT policy appendix on page 11.
  3. "Productivity software" sold to your employer is almost always surveillance software rebranded.
This is not to make you paranoid. It's to make you informed. The clause is usually in the IT policy appendix — the one nobody reads.
