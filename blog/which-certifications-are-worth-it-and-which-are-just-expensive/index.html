Which Certifications Are Worth It, and Which Are Just Expensive

Certificates rarely get you hired on their own. Some are still worth the time. Here is how to tell the difference before you pay.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 20 June 2026 6 min read

The hope is understandable: a credential that makes the decision for you. It mostly does not work that way, but some certifications genuinely help. The difference is predictable.

The test

A certification is worth doing if it does at least one of these:

  1. Named in job listings you actually want. Search ten. If it appears in three or more, it is real.
  2. Teaches a tool you will use daily. The learning is the value; the certificate is the receipt.
  3. Produces something shareable. A project, a portfolio piece, a dashboard.
  4. Is a genuine gate. Rare outside regulated fields, but real where it applies.

If none apply, you are buying reassurance, not employability. That is an expensive feeling.

Generally worth the time

  • Tool certifications from the vendor — HubSpot, Google Analytics, Meta Blueprint, Xero, QuickBooks, Zendesk. Mostly free or cheap, directly named in listings, and they teach the actual interface.
  • Google career certificates — data analytics, project management, UX, IT support. Affordable, well structured, and they end in portfolio work. Strongest for someone changing field.
  • Cloud fundamentals — AWS or Azure entry level, if you are heading toward technical work.
  • Bookkeeping software certification — genuinely a hiring signal for remote finance roles.

Rarely worth it

  • Generic "digital marketing masterclass" certificates from unknown providers. No employer recognises the issuer.
  • Anything promising a job guarantee for a large upfront fee. Read what the guarantee actually requires of you — it is usually unmeetable.
  • Long, expensive courses in a field you have not tested. Do a free short course first. If you dislike the work, you have saved a fortune.
  • Soft-skill certificates. Leadership, communication, emotional intelligence. Nobody hires on these, and you cannot demonstrate them with a PDF.

The thing that beats every certificate

A finished piece of work. A recruiter choosing between a certificate in data analysis and a dashboard that answers a real question will choose the dashboard, every time, because it proves the thing the certificate only claims.

So if you do take a course, treat the project as the point and the certificate as a by-product.

Where to put it on your CV

A short Certifications section under Education. Name, issuer, year. No logos, no badges, no percentage scores. And on LinkedIn, use the Licenses and Certifications section rather than putting it in your headline — a headline crowded with credentials pushes out the keywords that get you found.

A reasonable plan

One vendor certification for the main tool in your target function, done in a fortnight, free or cheap. One project built with it. Then stop studying and start applying — because past a certain point, more learning is just a comfortable way of avoiding rejection.
