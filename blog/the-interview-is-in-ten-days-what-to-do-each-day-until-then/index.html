The Interview Is in Ten Days: What to Do Each Day Until Then

An interview date changes the work. A ten-day schedule that covers the role, your examples, the technology and the money question.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 19 November 2026 3 min read

You have a date. That is good news, and it also changes what you should be doing. Stop applying for a few days. For the next ten days, your job is to turn that one interview into an offer.

Days 10 to 8: understand what they are buying

  • Print the advert. Underline every requirement.
  • Read the company's website, its product pages and its last few posts. Write three sentences on what the company sells and to whom.
  • Look up your interviewers on LinkedIn. Note what they do, so your answers meet their concerns.
  • Write one sentence: "They are hiring this role because..." If you cannot finish it, read more.

Days 7 to 5: one example for each requirement

For every underlined requirement, write a true example from your own work. Use four lines: the situation, what you were asked to do, what you did, and what changed.

Say each one aloud and time it. Two minutes is the limit. Most people need three attempts before an example sounds natural.

Prepare these too, because they are asked almost every time.

  • Tell me about yourself. Ninety seconds, ending on why this role.
  • Why do you want to work remotely? Talk about how you work, not about traffic.
  • Tell me about a mistake. Choose a real one, and say what you changed.
  • How do you manage your time without supervision?

Day 4: the practical test

Many remote employers set a short task or a trial. Ask the recruiter what the format is. If there is a task, find out the tools involved and try them now, not on the day.

Day 3: technology and room

  • Test the exact platform, whether Zoom, Meet or Teams, on the device you will use.
  • Arrange backup power and a second internet source. A phone hotspot is enough.
  • Choose a quiet spot with light on your face and a plain wall behind you.
  • Check the time zone twice. Put the interview in your calendar in WAT.

Day 2: the money question

Decide your number before they ask. Find the range for the role, set your floor, and practise one sentence: "Based on the role and my experience, I am looking for around X a month. Is that within your range?" Then stop talking.

Ask how and in what currency you would be paid. We covered the detail in The Money Conversation.

Day 1: a full rehearsal

Have someone ask you the questions for forty minutes, on camera. Record it and watch it once. Fix the two worst things and leave the rest alone.

Prepare three questions of your own. Good ones are about the work: what the first ninety days look like, how the team communicates, what success in the role is.

The day itself

Join five minutes early. Keep your one-page notes beside the screen. If the power fails or the line drops, rejoin calmly and say what happened. Interviewers who hire remotely have seen it before. How you handle it tells them something useful.

Send a short thank-you note the same day, with one specific thing you enjoyed discussing.
