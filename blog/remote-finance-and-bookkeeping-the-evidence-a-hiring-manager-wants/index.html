Remote Finance and Bookkeeping: The Evidence a Hiring Manager Wants

Finance and bookkeeping roles make up a steady share of the remote roles open to Africa, and they are hired almost entirely on proof of accuracy. How to turn local experience into evidence a distant hiring manager trusts.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 20 October 2026 6 min read

Finance and bookkeeping make up a steady share of the remote roles open to applicants in Africa, from accounts payable and receivable to payroll and treasury support. They share one hiring question: can we trust this person with our numbers without watching them?

What counts as evidence

Volume and accuracy, together. “Processed invoices” says nothing. “Processed around 300 supplier invoices a month with month-end reconciliation completed by day three” says almost everything a hiring manager needs.

Named systems. The accounting software, the spreadsheet functions, the ERP. If you have used it, name it. If you have not, learn the free version and say so honestly.

Controls. Did you introduce a check, catch an error, shorten a close, clear a backlog? Controls are what remote finance teams worry about most.

What to remove

Long lists of duties copied from a job description, and any figure that would identify a confidential client. Describe scale and outcome without exposing anyone.

The local-experience question

Local standards and local tax knowledge are not a weakness. Many global teams hire precisely because they need someone who understands African payments, currencies or suppliers. Say what you know, plainly, and do not apologise for where you learned it.

Building the document

Finance hiring managers read CVs the way they read ledgers: they look for what does not add up. A clean, readable, aligned CV matters more here than in most fields.
