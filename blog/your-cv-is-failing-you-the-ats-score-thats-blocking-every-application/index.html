Your CV is Failing You: The ATS Score That's Blocking Every Application

How to scan your CV the way hiring managers' software does — and the exact rewrite formula that takes you from 38% to 89% in under two hours.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 23 May 2026 8 min read

The Score Your CV Gets Before Any Human Sees It

Most job applications are scored by software — Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) — before a recruiter ever opens the file. The ATS compares your CV against the job description and assigns a match score. If you score below 65–70%, you are automatically rejected.

The brutal truth: the average Nigerian professional applying for a remote role scores between 30–45% on an ATS scan. Not because they're underqualified — but because their CV was formatted and written for a human reader, not a machine.

What ATS Systems Actually Check

  • Keyword density: Does your CV contain the exact words from the job description?
  • Formatting compatibility: Are there columns, tables, or text boxes that the parser can't read?
  • Section headers: Does the system recognise your section names (Work Experience, not "My Journey")?
  • Metrics and achievements: Does your CV show results or just list responsibilities?

The Achievement Bullet Formula

Every single bullet point on your CV should follow this formula:

Action Verb + What You Did + Result + Metric

Before: "Managed social media accounts and created content for the brand."

After: "Grew Instagram following by 2,300+ in 90 days through daily content strategy, increasing engagement rate from 1.2% to 4.7%."

The second version scores high because it contains: action verb, specific platform, specific numbers, time frame, and measurable outcome.

The 5 Formatting Mistakes That Kill Your ATS Score

  • Using a two-column layout (ATS reads left to right, misses right column content)
  • Putting your contact information in a header text box
  • Using graphics, logos, or icons in the CV body
  • Saving as .pages, .odt, or any format other than .docx or .pdf
  • Using creative section titles instead of standard ones
