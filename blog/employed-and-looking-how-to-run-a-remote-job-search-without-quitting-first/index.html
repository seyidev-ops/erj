Employed and Looking: How to Run a Remote Job Search Without Quitting First

You do not need to resign to search properly. A five-hour week, split into three kinds of work, is enough if you protect it.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 15 November 2026 3 min read

Many of the people we work with have a job. It pays in naira, it takes most of the week, and they want out of it. The question they ask is whether they have to resign to search properly.

No. Keep the salary. A search run from employment is stronger, because you can say no to a bad offer. What you need is a small plan that you actually keep.

The five-hour week

Five hours is enough if each hour has one job. Split them like this.

  • One hour of finding. One sitting, same day each week. Go through the Remote Job Board and your saved company pages. Pick five roles you are eligible for. Stop when you have five.
  • Three hours of applying. Three separate evenings or early mornings, one or two applications each. Tailor the top third of your CV to each advert.
  • One hour of repair. Fix one thing. A CV section this week, your LinkedIn headline next week, one interview example the week after.

The mistake is to spend all five hours scrolling for roles. That feels like searching and produces nothing you can send.

Protect the hours

An hour that depends on how you feel after work will not happen. Fix the days. Many people find 5:30 to 6:30 in the morning is the only hour nobody else can take. Others use Saturday before the house wakes up. Choose yours and tell the people you live with.

Keep it away from your employer

  • Use your own laptop, your own email and your own data. Never the office ones.
  • Do not update your LinkedIn with a burst of changes in one day. Spread the edits over two weeks, and turn off the setting that announces profile changes to your network.
  • Take interview calls outside working hours where you can. Global employers are used to arranging this across time zones, so ask.
  • Keep doing your current job well. Your present manager may be the reference a new employer calls.

Count, do not guess

Write down three numbers each week: applications sent, replies received, interviews booked. After four weeks the numbers tell you where the problem is.

  • Few applications sent: you have a time or a supply problem.
  • Many sent, no replies: the CV, or the roles you are choosing.
  • Interviews, no offers: the last step, which is a skill you can practise.
