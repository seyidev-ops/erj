Tax and the Remote Worker in Nigeria: Five Questions to Ask Before January

Nigeria’s new tax law has been in force since January 2026. If you earn from abroad, or plan to, these are the questions to put to a tax professional.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 14 November 2026 3 min read

Nigeria's new tax law took effect in January 2026, and it has been widely reported that it reaches people who live here and earn from employers or clients abroad. If a remote role is your goal, this affects the number you should ask for and the records you should keep.

We train people for remote work. We are not tax advisers, and nothing here is tax advice. What follows is a summary of what has been published, and the questions worth taking to someone qualified.

What has been reported

Guides published this year, including one from Techpoint Africa in May 2026, describe the position broadly like this:

  • Residency decides it. If you live in Nigeria for more than half the year, or keep your home and family here, you are generally treated as tax resident.
  • Residents are taxed on worldwide income. That includes salary or fees paid by a company in another country.
  • The first 800,000 naira a year is reported as tax-free, with rates rising in bands above it.
  • You need a Tax Identification Number to file, and there are penalties for not registering.

Published guides do not all agree on the exact bands, so treat any table you find online with care and confirm figures with the tax authority or a professional.

Five questions to ask

  1. Am I an employee or a contractor? Most Nigerians hired by foreign companies are engaged as contractors. Nobody deducts tax for you, which means filing is your job.
  2. What counts as my income? Ask how foreign-currency payments are converted, and at what date.
  3. What can I deduct? Ask about the costs of doing the work, such as data, power and equipment, and what records you would need.
  4. When do I file, and how? Get the dates for the year in writing.
  5. Has tax already been taken abroad? If a platform or client withholds anything, ask how that is treated here.

What this changes about your search

Your rate. When a recruiter asks for your number, the figure you give is before tax. Work out what you need to take home, then add what you expect to pay, before you answer. We walk through the conversation in The Money Conversation.

Your records. Keep every contract, invoice and payment advice from the first month. Open a folder today. It costs nothing, and it is painful to rebuild later.

Your payment route. How you are paid affects what records exist. Settle it before you sign. See Getting Paid From Abroad.

Do not let this stop you

Tax is a sign that the income is real. People who earn locally pay it too. The difference for a remote worker is that nobody handles it on your behalf, so you plan for it. One hour with an accountant early in the year is cheaper than a penalty at the end of it.
