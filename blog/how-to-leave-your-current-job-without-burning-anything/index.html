How to Leave Your Current Job Without Burning Anything

The offer is signed. What happens over the next four weeks decides whether this person is a reference for the next decade or a problem.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 26 June 2026 5 min read

The remote world is smaller than it looks, and Nigerian professional networks are smaller still. The manager you leave badly turns up on a reference call in four years. Leaving well is not sentiment; it is self-interest.

Do not resign before the offer is written

Not after a verbal yes, not after a great final interview. Written offer, accepted in writing, start date confirmed. Verbal offers evaporate, and they do it most often at exactly the moment budgets are reviewed.

Tell your manager first, in a call

Not email, not a group, not a colleague who might mention it. Your direct manager, spoken, before anyone else knows.

"I wanted you to hear this from me first. I have accepted a role elsewhere and my last day would be [date]. I have thought about it carefully, and I want to leave everything in good shape."

Short, decided, no apology. If you sound uncertain you will spend a fortnight being negotiated with.

Then send the written notice

Three lines. Position, last day, thank you. No grievances, no explanation of why the new role is better. This document sits in a file for years and may be read by people who never met you.

On the counter-offer

It is common and it is usually the wrong thing to accept. The salary problem gets fixed; the reason you were looking generally does not. And you are now known to be someone who explored — which affects how you are treated at the next reorganisation.

If money genuinely was the only issue and the counter fixes it in writing, consider it. Otherwise decline warmly and move on.

The handover is where reputations are made

Most people mentally leave three weeks before they physically leave, and everyone notices. Spend the notice period doing the opposite:

  • Write down what only you know. Processes, passwords held properly, the supplier who only answers on WhatsApp, the report nobody else has run.
  • Finish what you can finish. Hand over what you cannot with a written status on each item.
  • Train your replacement if there is one, generously.
  • Introduce your successor to the people who matter, by name.

A good handover document is remembered longer than most of the work that preceded it.

Ask for the reference now

While the goodwill is fresh. "Would you be comfortable acting as a reference in future? And if you are willing, a few lines on LinkedIn would mean a lot." Ask in your final week, not two years later when they barely recall the detail.

Say nothing about the salary

Especially if the new one is dramatically higher. It creates resentment among people who cannot follow you and it travels further than you expect. "A remote role with an international team" is a complete answer.

The last day

Thank people individually rather than in a group message. Leave a contact address that is not your work email. Then go — and stay findable.
