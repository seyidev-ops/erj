Experienced but Invisible: Why Ten Years of Work Does Not Show on Your CV

A long career can read as a short list of duties. How to turn years of real work into lines a remote employer can check.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 13 November 2026 3 min read

Here is a pattern we see every week. Someone has eight or ten years of solid work behind them. They have run teams, closed accounts, kept operations alive through bad quarters. Their CV says "Responsible for managing day-to-day operations."

Nothing in that line is false. It is also the same line a person with one year of experience would write. The years are real, but they are not on the page.

Why this happens to experienced people

When you have done a job for a long time, the work feels obvious. You stop seeing it as an achievement, so you describe the job title's duties and not what you did with them. A local employer who knows your company can fill in the blanks. An employer in another country cannot. They have never heard of your company, so the line has to carry everything.

Duty against evidence

Compare these.

  • Duty: Responsible for customer complaints.
  • Evidence: Handled about 40 customer complaints a week by email and chat, and cut the average reply time from two days to six hours.
  • Duty: Managed the accounts team.
  • Evidence: Led a team of five that closed the monthly books by day four, down from day eleven.

The second line in each pair gives a stranger three things: how much, how often, and what changed because you were there.

How to dig the evidence out

You do not need to remember exact figures from 2019. For each role, ask yourself four questions and write down honest estimates.

  1. How much or how many? Customers, invoices, staff, branches, naira, reports.
  2. How often? Daily, weekly, every quarter.
  3. What got better? Faster, cheaper, fewer errors, more sales.
  4. Who relied on it? A director, a client, another department.

Use "about" where you are estimating. An honest "about 40 a week" is stronger than a vague "many", and it will stand up when an interviewer asks about it.

Put the remote signals in

A remote employer is also checking one more thing: can this person work without someone standing over them? You have probably done this already and never thought to mention it.

  • Did you report to a manager in another city or country?
  • Did you run projects over email, WhatsApp, Teams or Zoom?
  • Did you keep work moving when the office was closed?

Say so, in the bullet where it happened. "Reported weekly to a regional manager in Nairobi" tells them you have worked across distance before.

Cut what is costing you space

Ten years does not need four pages. Give the last two roles the most room, and shrink anything older than ten years to a line each. Remove the photo, the date of birth, the state of origin and the referees. They are not used abroad, and some of them confuse the software that reads your CV first.
