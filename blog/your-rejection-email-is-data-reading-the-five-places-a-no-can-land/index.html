Your Rejection Email Is Data: Reading the Five Places a No Can Land

A rejection is not a verdict on you. It is a timestamp. Where in the process the no arrived tells you which part of your search to repair, and the five positions need five different fixes.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 10 September 2026 7 min read

Two people get twelve rejections each. One concludes the market is closed to Nigerians. The other reads the timestamps, finds that eleven of the twelve arrived before a human had read anything, and fixes a formatting problem in an afternoon.

The difference is not optimism. It is treating the rejections as data with a position attached.

The five positions

Take your last twenty applications. For each, write where the process stopped. There are only five places, and each means something different.

  • Position 1, no response at all. Not a rejection. Silence is the response that teaches you nothing, and it usually means the file was never read by a person. Points at representation, or at companies that could never have hired you.
  • Position 2, an automated rejection inside seventy-two hours. A screen ran and you failed a stated condition: work authorisation, location, a certification, a knockout question answered wrongly. Almost always preventable at the research stage.
  • Position 3, rejection after a recruiter call. You were read, you were interesting enough to call, and fifteen minutes lost it. Usually salary, availability, notice period, or an answer that raised a risk you did not then close.
  • Position 4, rejection after a task or technical stage. Your claims and your demonstrated work did not match, or they matched and somebody else matched better. The most honest signal in the set.
  • Position 5, rejection at final stage. You were a real candidate. Nothing about your search is broken. This is where people quit, and it is the worst place to quit from.

The counting that changes your mind

Count the distribution, not the total.

Sixteen of twenty in position 1 is not a rejection problem. It is a document problem or a targeting problem, and no amount of applying will move it. More effort at the wrong point is a louder version of the same silence.

Most in position 2? Spend an hour reading the knockout questions on your last five application forms. You will usually find the same wrong answer repeated.

Clustering at 3 and 4 is good news, genuinely. Three of the four points already work. Your CV parses, your aim is right, roles reach you. What is left is the one thing you cannot see about yourself.

And if you have never reached position 3, no interview coaching will help this month, because you do not yet have the problem it solves.

The distinction people get wrong

Rejection and silence are not the same event. A rejection means a human read you and decided, which is a representation success. Silence means the file never arrived anywhere a decision could be made. People treat both the same, feel equally bad about both, then repair whichever they feel worst about, usually rewriting a CV that was already working.

If you are getting interviews, stop rewriting your CV. It is the part that works.
