That "63% of Global Firms Hire From Africa" Statistic, Read Properly

The number is real, the survey is a year old, and the sample is 67 companies. What it genuinely tells a job seeker in Lagos, and what it does not.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 23 September 2026 7 min read

A statistic has been circulating in African remote-work circles: 62.9% of international companies are already hiring remote workers from Africa. It gets quoted as proof that the door is open.

It comes from the Future of Remote Work in Africa report, published by Rayda in September 2025 — a year old now. Before you plan a career around it, it is worth reading what was actually measured.

What the study was

Sixty-seven companies were surveyed, across seven developed countries, weighted heavily towards the United States (57%) and the United Kingdom (24%). Separately, a thousand remote employees were surveyed across Egypt, South Africa, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria and Rwanda.

Sixty-seven companies is a small sample, and companies that answer a survey about hiring in Africa are more likely to be companies that hire in Africa. That is not a criticism of the researchers; it is how surveys work, and it is why the honest reading of “63%” is of those surveyed, not of the world.

What is genuinely useful in it

The composition is more useful than the headline. Among those hiring, the sectors were technology (42.9%), finance (18.4%), consulting (12.2%), education (9.2%) and healthcare (6.1%). The skills most sought were software development (48.3%), UI/UX design (41.7%), data science (21%) and product management (11.6%).

The pay bands, monthly: $500–$1,000 for one to three years of experience, $1,000–$2,000 for three to six, and $3,000–$7,000 at senior level.

Those bands have held up. They match what we see in offers, and they are the honest range to plan against — not the screenshots of $8,000 packages that circulate on social media, which are real and rare in the same way lottery wins are real and rare.

The barrier nobody wants to hear

The employees surveyed named their own obstacles: unreliable electricity (37%) and inconsistent internet (33%).

Not skill. Not qualifications. Not racism. Power and bandwidth.

That is worth sitting with, because it is the one item on this list you can act on this week for a known amount of money, and because it is the thing most likely to end a job you have already won. A backup connection and a power buffer are not luxuries in this line of work; they are the equipment.

What this changes about your search

Three things, concretely.

Target the sectors that actually hire. If your field is nowhere near that list, your route to a global remote role is longer and the honest plan is different from the one in the motivational posts.

Plan against $500–$2,000, not against a screenshot. An entry-to-mid global role at today’s rates is life-changing money in Lagos without needing to be a fantasy.

Fix the infrastructure before the interview, not after the offer. A dropped video call in a final round costs more than an inverter.

And treat every statistic in this field, including the ones we publish, the same way: ask the sample size, ask the date, ask who was asked. The rest of our research posts are written to survive that test.
