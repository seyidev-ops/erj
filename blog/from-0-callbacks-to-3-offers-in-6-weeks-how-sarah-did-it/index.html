From 0 Callbacks to 3 Offers in 6 Weeks: How Sarah Did It

Sarah was applying to 60 remote jobs a month, for 5 months. One interview to show for it. Here's the exact 6-week process that turned it into 3 offers — and a $24,000 raise.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 24 April 2026 7 min read

The Starting Point

Sarah was applying to 60 remote jobs a month. For five months. And had received exactly one interview.

She came frustrated, convinced the market was the problem. It wasn't.

The Before Picture

  • CV last updated in 2019, formatted for job duties not achievements
  • Cover letters were generic ("I am writing to express my interest in…")
  • Applying only through job boards — never direct to companies
  • LinkedIn profile set to private
  • No portfolio — just a CV

The Six-Week Process

Week 1: CV Transformation

Rewrote her CV from job-duties format to achievement format. Every single bullet had a number. Before: "Managed social media accounts." After: "Grew LinkedIn following by 340% in 4 months, generating 12 qualified leads in Q3 alone."

Week 2: Portfolio Build

Built a three-page portfolio on Notion. Free. Done in a day. Three case studies showing her best work with before/after metrics.

Week 3: Target Company Research

Identified 12 target companies that were remote-first and actively hiring. Found the right hiring manager or team lead at each. Wrote 12 personalised outreach messages — each referencing something specific about the company.

Week 4: Follow-Up and Calls

Followed up on the 7 that opened her messages. Booked 4 discovery calls. Each call opened with a one-page brief of how she'd approach their biggest current challenge.

Weeks 5–6: Interview Preparation

Prepared for interviews using the STAR method with remote-specific scenarios. Practised answering "how do you manage your productivity without oversight?" and "walk me through your async communication approach."

The Results

Three job offers. One at $72,000 — up from her previous $48,000 in-office role. She accepted the role offering full flexibility and a $24,000 annual raise.

"I didn't know if it would work. Now I can't believe I spent 5 months doing it the other way." — Sarah

What This Actually Means

The market isn't broken. The old playbook is. Sarah is not a special case — she is the rule when the approach changes. If her story sounds like your last 3–6 months, the inputs need to change, not the effort.
