The Video Interview: Camera, Sound, and the Two-Minute Answer

Most people prepare what to say and ignore how they arrive. Both are fixable in an afternoon. Here is the checklist and the answer format.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 27 July 2026 5 min read

A video interview is judged on two things that have nothing to do with each other: whether you are easy to receive, and whether your answers are structured. Both are learnable and most candidates work on neither.

The forty-minute setup fix

  • Light in front, not behind. Face a window or put a lamp behind your screen. A window behind you turns you into a shadow.
  • Camera at eye level. Stack books under the laptop. Looking down at a camera is unflattering and reads as low confidence.
  • Wired headphones with a microphone. Bluetooth drops and compresses. Wired is more reliable and sounds better.
  • Plain wall behind you. Real beats virtual.
  • Test the actual platform. Zoom, Meet and Teams all behave differently. Open the one they sent and check camera and mic ten minutes before, not on the call.
  • Close everything else. Notifications appear when you share your screen.

Record two minutes of yourself and watch it back. It is uncomfortable and it will show you three fixable things immediately.

The answer format

Interviewers ask "tell me about a time when..." because they want evidence, not opinions. The reliable structure is four parts:

  1. Situation — one sentence of context
  2. Task — what you specifically were responsible for
  3. Action — what you actually did, in steps. This is the longest part.
  4. Result — what changed, with a number where you have one

Aim for about ninety seconds to two minutes. Under thirty seconds sounds thin. Over three minutes and they stop following.

An example

"At the fintech I supported, our first-response time had drifted to about eight hours and complaints were rising. I owned the support queue. I looked at three weeks of tickets and found that around 60 percent were the same five issues, so I wrote template replies for those and reorganised the queue by issue type instead of arrival time. Within six weeks average first response was under two hours and satisfaction went from the low 70s to above 90."

Situation, task, action, result. Under ninety seconds. Contains numbers.

Prepare six stories, not thirty answers

Most behavioural questions are variations on the same themes. Write out six stories in this format:

  • A time you fixed something that was broken
  • A time you handled a difficult person
  • A time you missed something or got it wrong
  • A time you worked with people you could not see
  • A time you had to learn something quickly
  • A time you managed your own priorities without supervision

Six stories will cover almost anything they ask. Practise them out loud, not in your head — the gap between the two is larger than you expect.

Have three questions ready

"What does success look like in this role at ninety days?" "How does the team handle the time-zone spread?" "What is the biggest thing you are hoping this person fixes?"

Asking nothing signals that you have not thought about the job.

Afterwards

Send a four-line note the same day: thank them, name one specific thing from the conversation, restate briefly why you fit, and say you are happy to provide anything else. Most candidates skip this. It costs three minutes.
