The Interview You Have in January Starts in December

Most people prepare for an interview after it is booked. The best preparation happens before, when there is no pressure. Here is what to do over the holiday.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 23 December 2026 3 min read

When an interview invitation arrives, most people have a few days to prepare, and they spend them anxious. The preparation that makes the biggest difference is the part you can do now, before anything is booked, when nobody is waiting for you.

Build five stories

Most interview questions, however they are worded, ask for evidence. Tell me about a time you solved a problem. Tell me about a difficult person. Tell me about a mistake. If you have five strong stories from your work, you can answer most of them.

Choose five moments from your work where you:

  1. Solved a problem nobody else had solved.
  2. Delivered something under pressure or with little time.
  3. Handled a difficult person or conversation.
  4. Made a mistake and fixed it.
  5. Improved how something was done.

For each, write: the situation in one sentence, what you did, and what happened as a result, with a number if you have one. Keep each one under two minutes when spoken.

Prepare the two questions everyone gets

"Tell me about yourself." Ninety seconds. Who you are professionally, the one or two things you are best at, with evidence, and why this kind of role is your next step. Then stop.

"Why remote, and how do you work remotely?" A concrete answer: your workspace and backup power, your hours of overlap with their time zone, how you report your progress. Not "I am disciplined".

Know your money numbers

Spend thirty minutes looking at pay figures for your target role in adverts, then set your floor, target and stretch. Decide now how you will answer "what are your salary expectations?" so you do not decide in the moment.

Record yourself once

This is the step most people skip, and the most useful. Record yourself on your phone answering "tell me about yourself" and one story. Watch it. You will notice things no one else will tell you: the filler words, the story that takes too long to get to the point, the result you forgot to mention. Fix them and record again.

Check your setup

Test your camera, microphone, light and internet now. Know what you will do if the power or connection fails mid-call: a charged phone with data, and a short message ready to send to the interviewer.
