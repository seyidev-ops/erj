If You Read Nothing Else This Month, Read This

Everything from the last three weeks, compressed into the eight things that actually decide whether you get hired.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 29 June 2026 5 min read

Three weeks of posts, reduced to what actually moves the outcome. If you do nothing else, do these.

1. Check the location line before anything else

"Remote, US only" means it. Reading that one line first will cut a third of your list and save more hours than any other habit.

2. Rebuild the CV as one column with numbers in it

Software reads it before a human does, and it cannot handle columns, tables, text boxes or headers. Then turn every duty into an achievement with a figure. An honest estimate is fine; no number is not.

3. Make yourself findable

Public profile, headline with your job title and two tools and the word remote, Skills section filled, Open to Work switched on with Remote selected. A private profile is invisible no matter how good you are.

4. Build three pieces of proof this weekend

Real work de-identified, or small things you build yourself. Three sentences of context around each: the problem, what you did, what changed. Test the link in a private browser window.

5. Twelve applications beat sixty

Each application is an argument that you can do a job, not a lottery ticket. Twelve careful ones with the CV adjusted to each listing, plus five direct messages to named hiring managers, is a working week.

6. Reach the person, not just the portal

Apply properly first, then send six sentences to the person who owns the role. One result with a number, one specific true observation, one tiny ask. It takes ten minutes per role and it is where most replies come from.

7. Prepare six stories and the logistics answer

Six situation-task-action-result stories cover almost every behavioural question. Separately, prepare specifics for the infrastructure and overlap questions — two connections, inverter hours, the fallback, and the actual time-zone arithmetic.

8. Expect eight to sixteen weeks, and diagnose from where it breaks

No replies means the CV. Replies but no interviews means proof. Interviews but no second round means the interview itself. Final stages but no offer means you are already competitive — keep going.

The honest note to end on

Everything above is free and every step can be done alone. Most people who stall do so at the same two places: writing about themselves, and believing they have nothing worth showing. Both are outside-eye problems rather than effort problems, which is why they are so hard to solve by trying harder.

If you get through all eight of these on your own, you do not need us. If you stall, that is not a character flaw — it is the normal result of trying to describe yourself without a second pair of eyes.
