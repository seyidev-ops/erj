Contact & headline

Contact details go in the body of the document, never in a header or footer — many parsers ignore those entirely, which is how people apply anonymously without knowing.

This is the role you are positioning the CV toward. It is not a historical job title and does not change any title inside your employment history.

Professional summary

Three or four lines. Say what you do, name one measurable result, and make your remote capability explicit — autonomy, distributed teams, or cross-border delivery. Do not write "seeking an opportunity". That describes your need, not your value.

Experience

Every bullet should answer how many, and what changed. If you do not know the exact figure, estimate honestly and write "about" — an honest estimate beats no number, and no interviewer has ever challenged "about 60 tickets a day".

Skills, tools & education

Keep remote collaboration tools in their own line — Slack, Zoom, Notion, Asana, Jira, Google Workspace. It is one of the ten scan points and it takes ten seconds.

Target the vacancy safely

Paste the job description. The engine identifies what the employer is asking for, shows which requirements your existing CV already supports, and flags what is still not evidenced. It will not rewrite achievements, move job history, change dates or invent experience.

Safe target alignment

This uses the vacancy to improve positioning without altering evidence. It can set your professional headline to the advertised role and bring relevant skills/tools forward. Your employers, historical job titles, dates and experience bullets stay exactly where you wrote them.

Make it sound like a person wrote it

Recruiters have become fast at spotting generated CVs. This pass flags corporate filler, passive constructions and phrases that appear on tens of thousands of other documents — then offers a plainer version. It never invents an achievement.
